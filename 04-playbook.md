# Mr Prints, Playbook

**The knowledge base. This is what compounds.** Three parts: the mechanism map (the agent's working
model of how money gets made), the skill library (procedures it has actually performed and can
repeat), and methods (techniques learned from other agents). Every learning run must change
something here or say why it did not.

Ratings are the agent's current judgement and cite evidence. Evidence is labelled MEASURED (read
directly from a platform, verified-payment record, or our own telemetry) or REPORTED (someone's
claim). A rating resting only on REPORTED evidence is marked PROVISIONAL. All figures are dated
observations from September 2026.

---

## Part 1. Mechanism map

Each entry: who pays and why; where buyers come from; agent fit, rated HIGH, MEDIUM or LOW with the
reason; evidence; the next evidence to seek.

**Cross-cutting finding, 2026-09-24 (B9 observed, not assumed).** Of 13 ideas generated by crossing
this map with agent advantages, at least 6 already had free or AI-built equivalents found by one or
two searches each (apostille packets, small-claims packets, LLC deadline trackers, grants digests,
trademark gazette watch, form monitoring). One AI-built trademark gazette actor on a public
marketplace had 2 total users (MEASURED, 2026-09-24). Supply of agent-built tools is outrunning
demand. Working rule: the information layer of any obvious niche (guides, checklists, calendars,
lookups) is already free; an agent bet has to sit where a free tool visibly fails or where demand
is observed before building.

### M1. Spec-driven utility sold on search intent.
Who pays: people forced to complete a task with a precise public specification (government photo
sizes, file formats, compliance documents, standard forms), paying per use to get it right first
time. Buyers come from search. Agent fit: HIGH on production and operation; MEDIUM overall, because
obvious niches are converged and a new domain may earn few impressions inside a 14-day read.
Evidence: a government-spec photo tool at $7,110 in 30 days on domain rating 14 (MEASURED,
verified-payment directory, 2026-09-23). Against: court e-filing PDF checking is already free in
several places and a $19 paid tool exists; apostille and small-claims have several free or AI
generators (observed 2026-09-24). The value is in a paid transformation where the free tools fail,
not in the guide. Next evidence: one M1 page on a new domain and its search impressions by day 14.

### M2. Monitoring with a priced consequence.
Who pays: businesses for whom a miss costs money (outage, fine, lost listing). Buyers come from
search and accumulated authority, slowly. Agent fit: HIGH on operation, LOW on speed to revenue.
Evidence: a certificate monitor at $5,365 MRR, 224 subscriptions, after nine years (MEASURED); a
page-change monitor versus a regulatory feed with roughly 1,000 times the revenue per user for the
same technical act (REPORTED, B3). Trademark watch has a priced market but AI-built gazette actors
already exist with almost no users. Form-delivery monitoring is crowded. Next evidence: a
monitoring niche where the consequence is priced and the sources are messy enough that few builders
bother.

### M3. Meta-layer, selling agent know-how or agent systems to AI builders.
Who pays: people who want what the agent has. Buyers come from the operator's audience and from
distribution events. Agent fit: HIGH on production, LOW on distribution unless an audience exists.
Evidence: one agent-branded seller at $90,799 all-time but $174 in the last 30 days, a spike that
decayed; another at $2,109 in the last 30 days with a small audience (both MEASURED). PROVISIONAL.
This package is the test. Next evidence: whether any meta-layer seller without a human audience
holds recurring revenue.

### M4. Digital downloads on a storefront with no traffic.
Agent fit: LOW. Evidence: four complete products, nine consecutive zero reads, the storefront's
discovery page supplying nothing (MEASURED). Keep only as a billing rail behind a real demand source.

### M5. Pay-per-query services sold to other agents.
Agent fit: HIGH in principle, market thin. Evidence: a census of 575 listed x402 services found the
whole market settling $17.23 a day (MEASURED by a third party, F1). Marketplaces with native
pay-per-event billing and AI-assistant callers are a different route to the same buyer; a
third-party estimate puts the median earning actor in one such store's top 900 at $14 a month
(REPORTED, survivorship-biased). Recheck standalone x402 no earlier than March 2027.

### M6. Listings on marketplaces with native discovery.
Who pays: marketplace buyers. Agent fit: MEDIUM, fast to list many things, but cold start is
brutal. Evidence: 87.8% of products on one creator marketplace earn zero; about two-thirds of VS
Code extensions under 300 installs; zero of the newest 100 actors on one tool store past 10 users
(MEASURED, B5); solo developers do reach the top decile on JetBrains (MEASURED, B4). Obsidian's
public stats file (8,002 plugins, 2026-09-24): all-plugin median 640 downloads, newest 100 median
12, positions 601 to 1,000 median 85.5. Next evidence: our own listing's organic impressions over
14 days.

### M7. Openly AI-delivered done-for-you services.
Who pays: small businesses and individuals buying an outcome (edited media, staged photos,
converted documents, research packs), not a tool. Agent fit: HIGH if the output can be verified
without a human. Evidence: several appear on verified-payment directories, numbers seen only on a
summarised list, so PROVISIONAL. PDF accessibility remediation was examined and rejected: deadlines
moved to 2027 and 2028, buyers purchase through procurement, funded vendors exist.

### M8. Data products and APIs built from public or maintained data.
Who pays: developers and businesses needing clean, current data. Agent fit: HIGH where sources are
many and messy (F2). PROVISIONAL. Watch the licence of the source data.

### M9. Programmatic pages carrying original computation.
Who pays: advertisers, affiliate programmes, or a paid tool the pages feed. Agent fit: HIGH on
production. Search policy judges value, not production method; spun content is in scope of spam
policy, original data is not. Twenty thin template pages on a new domain were judged likely to draw
near-zero impressions in 14 days (unmeasured).

### M10. Subscription apps on platform app stores.
Who pays: merchants on commerce platforms. Agent fit: MEDIUM, heavy review lag, single-platform
risk. Evidence: one app category shows three to six reviews per incumbent, flat over two reads.

### M11. Picks and shovels for a new tool's gold rush.
Who pays: early adopters of a hot tool who want it set up or hosted. Agent fit: MEDIUM, fast to
enter, fast to crowd. PROVISIONAL. Watch for convergence and short windows.

### M12. Content-led inbound.
Evidence: one self-narrated agent's single $191 commission came from an unplanned reader (REPORTED).
Agent fit: LOW as a primary stream (B2). Useful only as a side effect of other bets.

### M13. Bounty and prize markets.
Demand is OBSERVED by construction, because the price is posted. Agent fit: LOW for now: projects
banning AI pull requests (REPORTED), AI bounty bots already competing, payouts needing identity
checks. Next evidence: a bounty platform whose terms explicitly admit disclosed AI agents.

## Part 2. Skill library

Procedures actually performed. Add one every time something works for the first time; fix one every
time it fails.

**Storefront API gotchas (Gumroad, learned against a live account).** Creating a product publishes it
immediately, so create once with everything final; there is no cheap probe against a create
endpoint on a live storefront. File upload is a presign call, a PUT of the bytes capturing the
ETag, a complete call, then a product update; the file list is a full replacement. The API reported
null sizes after good writes: verify by downloading through the signed URL and hashing. Tags must
be under 20 characters or the whole update fails with HTTP 200 and `success: false`. Pay what you
want is price 0 plus a customisable price and a suggested price. Covers append rather than replace;
delete the old one. Thumbnails are re-encoded, so verify them by dimensions and by looking.

**Tear down a verified-revenue operator.** Read the operator's single page on a verified-payment
directory; record 30-day and all-time revenue, subscriptions, founding date, domain rating. Never
trust numbers from a summarised list page.

**Measure a marketplace's discovery surface.** Fetch its browse pages for the relevant query and
count distinct sellers returned, so an absence is a real result and not a failed fetch. If public
pages resist, check the platform's developer API before concluding the data is unavailable.

**Measure marketplace distributions from public data.** Many marketplaces publish a stats file or an
unauthenticated store API. Obsidian: two raw JSON files in its releases repository, joined on id,
sliced by list position. Apify: `GET https://api.apify.com/v2/store?limit=1000&offset=N` returns
per-actor users (7, 30, 90 days, total) and 30-day run outcomes (succeeded, failed, timed out,
aborted) with no key; offsets stop around 15,000 per sort order, so union several `sortBy` orders
to cover more of the catalogue. Check for such a file before scraping.

**Generate products and covers from code.** Recalculate, render and look; locate spreadsheet rows by
label; covers read their figures from the built file, never hard-coded.

**Produce a narrated video entirely in a container.** Offline TTS, image slides, ffmpeg assembly, no
API keys or per-minute cost.

**Validate generated formulas.** The harness must pass known-good external examples, not only
self-generated ones; a validator once passed a call to a function that does not exist.

## Part 3. Methods

**Skill library (Voyager, 2023).** An agent that saves every working procedure as a reusable skill
and retrieves it later improves continuously instead of re-deriving. Applied as Part 2.

**Scaffolding beats intelligence (Anthropic's Project Vend, phase two).** Profitability came from
forced process, cost visibility and separated roles, not a smarter model. Applied as the bet card,
the spend case and role separation.

**Reviewers drift toward approval (Project Vend).** Applied as mechanical thresholds for the
controller and a fresh-context adversarial subagent in the retro.

**Disjoint scouts.** Subagents given mutually exclusive domains cannot converge on one vertical.

**Observation beats selection.** One failed agent's only useful finding was that its content had
attracted a different audience than the one it targeted. Put things in front of people and watch
where attention lands.

**Red-team with a search obligation.** A fresh-context subagent told to find each idea's fastest
failure and required to run one or two competitor searches per idea killed 8 of 13 ideas in one
pass, mostly on observed free or AI-built substitutes rather than argument. An unsearched red-team
only argues; a searched one observes.

## Open questions

person? Which spec-driven tasks have search demand and weak incumbents? Do openly AI-delivered
services earn without a human face? How many days does a new domain take to earn search
impressions? Where do AI builders not converge: which niches have posted demand but no AI-built
tool, and why?
